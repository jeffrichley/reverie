# What arriving feels like

**A prototype, not a spec.** Written to be reacted to and expected to be wrong in places. A
concrete wrong thing is faster to correct than an abstract right question, so every beat below
is a call somebody made rather than a thing that has been decided. The last section lists the
calls most likely to be wrong.

The world is a Survivor season: eighteen castaways on an island, a game with votes in it. You
authored it three weeks ago and you have been away seven days. You left it running.

---

## I. What you are told

You open it on a Sunday night.

Seven days ran. Four hundred and eleven things happened. Two people are gone — one voted out,
one walked into the sea at three in the morning, which the world holds as ground truth and
which nobody on the island holds at all. They think she quit.

None of that is what it leads with. What it leads with is one line:

> **Nine people believe Dev had the idol. Dev did not have the idol.**

That is the whole reason to come back. The events are cheap. What people made of them is the
thing you came for, and the arrival is written in those terms or it is a changelog.

Underneath, in smaller type, the shape of the week: five of the eighteen were awake for most
of it. The other thirteen had a week — a name, a job, a clock, a place at the fire — with
nobody home. Mara was awake for the part that mattered, which is the only reason the week has
a story in it at all.

## II. What actually happened

Day two, morning. Priya found the idol under the third marker and told nobody. That is ground
truth and it stays ground truth for the rest of the week.

Day two, afternoon. Mara saw Dev come up from the ravine with his sleeves wet and worked it
out. She was wrong. She told Cass that night, and she told him sincerely, because she believed
it — she was not lying, she was mistaken out loud.

Cass told Ben. Ben told Silas while the two of them were filling bottles, and nobody else was
close enough to hear, so for a day and a half it was four people and not five. By day six it
was nine. Three of them could tell you exactly who told them. Two of them will tell you they
worked it out themselves, and they are not lying either; they genuinely do not remember being
told.

Day six they voted Dev out. On the way down the path he said someone had set him up. He
believes that. It is also not true. There was nobody.

Priya still has it. She is going to play it on day nine and it is going to work.

**Nobody lied.** One woman was wrong out loud and the island did the rest of it without help,
in seven days, while you were not there. That is the thing you are being shown.

## III. What you do

**You read it first, and reading costs nothing.** You follow the chain backwards from the vote
to Mara's wet sleeves and nobody feels watched, because there is nothing to feel — no shiver,
no record, no way for any of them to ever come to know you were looking. You spend twenty
minutes inside nine people's heads and leave no fingerprint on the world at all.

**You want to rewind and you don't.** You could put Dev back on the island. The undo exists
and it works. But the rewind is written down, permanently, in a place only you can see, and
what you would be spending it on is a week that came out better than anything you would have
written. So you leave the scar unmade and let it stand.

Later, out of curiosity, you ask what would have happened if you had not walked in at all. You
run it. It is worse. You do not tell anyone this because there is nobody to tell.

**Then you go in.** Not to find out anything — you already know everything, including what
Priya is going to do on day nine and why. You go in because Ben is going to ask her a question
at the fire tonight, and knowing what she would say is not the same as having to say it.

So you are Priya for eleven minutes. You have her hands and her sunburn and only what she
knows, which is less than what you know and that is the whole point. Ben asks whether she
thinks Dev deserved it. You have to answer. You answer badly — you are a little too kind about
Dev, because you know he was innocent and she does not, and the kindness does not fit the
woman she has been all week.

Then you leave, and she keeps it.

**Two other things, small.** You make the supply boat late on day nine, which is not something
anybody did; boats are late. And you pin one thing: Silas's infected hand does not kill him.
Everything you did not pin, the world is still free to fill in however it likes — it just may
not overturn the hand.

## IV. What it cost

Ben has been turning it over since the fire. Priya defended Dev. Priya has never defended
anybody. By morning Ben believes Priya was working with Dev, which is false, and which he came
to honestly from something he really did witness.

You made that. Not the simulation — you, in her mouth, for eleven minutes.

And if you ask Priya tomorrow why she said it, she will have a reason. A good one, that hangs
together, that she believes completely. She will never find out it was not hers. That is the
default, and it is the default because it is what people actually do; a world that would rather
leave her a hole where the reason should be can choose that instead.

There is no undo for this except the one with the scar on it. **The author is just another
source of false belief in the world, and the only way to take it back is a thing you can never
take back.**

Somewhere in the corner: the floor is set at six, and at the rate this island is going the
season reaches it in twelve days. You have not decided what happens then.

## Where a call got made

The parts most likely to be wrong, and worth an argument each. **This is not the complete
list** — the ones that need changing are often not the ones anyone thought to write down.

1. **The arrival leads with a disagreement, not a summary.** *Nine people believe a false
   thing* is the headline and the four hundred happenings are footnotes. This is the biggest
   call in the draft: it says the product is what people made of events, not the events.

2. **The week's story has no villain in it.** Nobody lied; one honest mistake did all the
   damage. That is a deliberate choice to show the machinery working without anyone driving
   it. A version where Mara knew she was lying is a different and possibly better draft.

3. **Two of the nine do not remember being told** and believe they worked it out. That is a
   claim about how beliefs decay, and nothing has decided that they do.

4. **Five awake, thirteen scenery, and the draft does not say who chose.** Did you set that
   before you left? Did the world pick? Did it pick well — Mara happened to be awake for the
   moment that mattered, and if she had not been there would have been no week at all.

5. **Possession is for answering, not for knowing.** The draft argues you never need to be
   someone to find something out, because you already see everything. You need it for the
   moment somebody asks a question and it has to be answered in her mouth.

6. **Eleven minutes and out.** Possession here is short and surgical. Nothing says it could not
   be a week, and a week feels like a very different feature.

7. **You did not rewind.** The draft makes not rewinding the interesting choice and never shows
   what rewinding actually looks like. That may be dodging the harder scene.

8. **Replay got used for regret** — *what would have happened if I had not come* — rather than
   for debugging. That is probably what it is actually for.

9. **The floor is a background worry rather than a thing the world does.** Twelve days out, in
   the corner of the screen. It might need to be much louder, or not present at all.

10. **The whole thing is one screen and one evening.** Nothing here says what the second visit
    is like, and the second visit is the one that decides whether anybody keeps a world.
